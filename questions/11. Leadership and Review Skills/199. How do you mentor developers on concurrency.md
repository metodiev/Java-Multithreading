# 199. How do you mentor developers on concurrency?

> **Section:** Leadership and Review Skills · **Level:** Architect / Tech Lead

## TL;DR

Keep ownership with the developer while making the reasoning concrete. Ask them to explain shared state, invariants, and ownership; walk through an interleaving together; then compare the simplest safe options and agree on evidence that will validate the fix.

## Practical mentoring

Pair on a small code change or test, explaining why a proposed primitive fits the invariant. Give actionable review feedback: identify the exact operation that can interleave and suggest a safe pattern (for example, one atomic map operation or synchronization around the entire invariant). Let the developer present the design and evidence, then choose one next concept to deepen.

For production incidents, be blameless: review the system and process, then turn findings into preventive tests, better defaults, or clearer diagnostics. Encourage controlled use of stress tests, profilers, and thread dumps while being honest about their limits.

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    A[Developer describes state and invariant] --> B[Walk through adverse interleaving]
    B --> C[Compare simplest safe approaches]
    C --> D[Developer implements focused change]
    D --> E[Review test and operational evidence]
    E --> F[Reflect and choose next learning step]
```

## Pitfalls and decision checklist

- [ ] Feedback names the behavior and risk, not only “race condition.”
- [ ] The developer explains why the chosen fix is safe.
- [ ] Pairing and examples are matched to experience and context.
- [ ] Evidence limitations are explicit; a passing stress test is not proof.
- [ ] Incident learning improves systems rather than assigning blame.

Avoid taking over the implementation before the developer has had a chance to reason through the problem.
